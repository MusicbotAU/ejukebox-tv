On the day

What we would need from you.

Short list, and none of it is a project.

  • A spare HDMI port. On the TV you already have. The player is a compact box that plugs straight into it.
  • Wifi it can reach, or a network point near the TV. The network point is the sturdier of the two if the screen is a long way from the office.
  • Your logo. Whatever file you have. If the only copy is on a coaster, tell us and we will sort it out.
  • About ten minutes of somebody's time. On the day it goes in. Not ten minutes a week afterwards - it looks after itself once it is running.

Straight answers

The questions we actually get asked.

Including the one about money, which is at the bottom and answered as honestly as we can answer it before we have seen your room.

Will it work with my TV?

Almost certainly. If the screen has a spare HDMI port, the player goes into it the same way a set-top box does. It powers on with the venue and runs around the clock. If your setup is unusual - an old panel, a projector, a screen already fed by something else - tell us what it is and we will tell you straight whether it will work.

What if the internet drops?

The player is built to keep the screen up on its own. A watchdog restarts the display if anything stops it, and the fleet is monitored at our end so a problem gets spotted here rather than found by your staff on a Saturday night.

Anything new - a fresh guest photo, a slide you just typed - has to come down the connection, so that is the part that waits. If the wifi at the far end of the room is patchy, say so on the call and we will look at a network point near the TV instead.

Who decides what photos go up?

You do, and the AI screens everything first. Every guest photo is checked for nudity, offensive gestures and anything tasteless before it can reach the wall. What does not pass is held back, not displayed - it never gets as far as the screen.

On top of that, the manager app lets you approve or hide photos one at a time or in bulk, push notifications tell you when something needs a decision, and you can stop the whole show from your phone from anywhere.

Can a staff member break it?

Not easily. The TV is PIN-locked - press Escape at the screen and you get a PIN pad, not a desktop, not a file manager, not somebody's browser tabs. There is nothing to click out of and nothing to accidentally close.

Staff sign in without passwords, using Google, Microsoft or Apple, organised into per-venue teams. You add a manager by email and remove someone the day they leave.

Do I need to buy new screens?

No. That is the whole point of it - it runs on the screens you already own and already pay the power on. A spare HDMI port is the requirement. If you later want a second screen, or a portrait one by the door, it slots into the same system and gets its own version of every photo.

Does it need the music system too?

No. The screen stands on its own and plenty of rooms would only ever want the screen.

If you run eJukebox music as well, the two become one thing: the same QR code that puts a photo on the wall takes song requests, and at a function the host controls cover the music as well as the screen. The music side lives at ejukebox.com.au.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many screens you have and how the room is set up - one TV behind a bar is a different job from four screens, a function room and a portrait panel at the door.

So rather than put a number on a web page that turns out to be wrong for your venue, tell us about the room and we will price it properly. It takes one short conversation and you get a figure that actually holds.
