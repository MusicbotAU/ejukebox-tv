One real night, this month

Changing the channel in a pub today.

The owner went to put the game on. This is how it went, and how it goes in a lot of venues on a Friday.

✕The aerial drops out

Free-to-air on the bar TV breaks up into blocks mid-game. Digital reception was never perfect, and when it goes, it goes in squares.

Sign-in failed

✕The box wants a login

Upstairs, the streaming box asks for the 7plus app to be signed in again. It wants a QR code scanned on a phone. It fails. Nobody knows the password.

✕The remote is flat

The finicky little remote will not change the station. Someone hunts for batteries. Everyone gives up and it gets changed by hand, on the TV itself.

✓

✓With eJukebox

One tap on the manager's phone. Every screen switches, upstairs and down. The sound comes on. The music goes down. Nobody moves.

By the time someone found batteries, the first quarter was gone and the music was still going under the commentary. That is a normal Friday in a lot of venues. With eJukebox it is one tap on the manager's phone: every screen switches, the sound comes up, the music goes down, and after the whistle the specials come back on the screens and the music comes back on its own.

Sound

Two lanes that never cross.

The question every venue asks first: does the commentary come through my music speakers? No. Never. The TV's sound comes out of the TV, the music stays on the music speakers, and each has its own volume on the phone.

Music

  1. Plays through the venue's music speakers, as it always has
  2. Goes quiet by itself when a game with sound starts
  3. Comes back where it was at the whistle
  4. Its own volume on the phone

TV

  1. Comes out of the TV, down the same lead as the picture
  2. Comes on at kick-off, if the game was set with sound
  3. Stays on under the ad-break slides, so the game comes straight back
  4. Its own volume on the phone

Rather keep the music going through the game? Set the game silent instead. On many TVs the phone can even change the volume of the TV itself.
