The run sheet

How a function actually runs.

One screen, one evening, nobody standing over it. This is a Saturday night engagement party in a function room, start to finish.

5:30 pm

Before anyone arrives

Their names are already on the screen, next to your logo. The room is set, the bar is stocked, and the first thing the family sees when they walk in is that somebody thought about them.

6:30 pm

Arrivals - the slideshow

The photos they sent through run as a slideshow while people find the bar and work out where they are sitting. Same screen. No laptop on a chair, no cable dangling off the end of the bar.

8:00 pm

The wall opens

QR cards on the tables. Somebody's aunt scans one to see what happens, and about three seconds later she is on the big screen. From then on it does not stop.

9:30 pm

Host mode

Whoever is running the night has the controls on their own phone. Skip straight to the song her dad asked for, at the moment he asks for it, without going looking for a staff member first.

10:00 pm

Speeches

The music stops cleanly at the end of a song, so the toast lands in a quiet room instead of over the top of a fade somebody pulled down halfway through a chorus.

11:30 pm

Last drinks

Typed at the bar in about eight seconds, designed by the time the phone is back in a pocket, up for half an hour, then gone on its own. Nobody has to remember to take it down.

Every photo that went up is kept, in both shapes, with your logo on it. The room is already being reset for a christening and the whole night is still sitting there.

Host mode

Give the host the keys, just for the night.

The person actually running the evening - the events coordinator, the best mate with the speeches, the brother who has appointed himself DJ - gets their own admin access for that night. Not your manager's login, and not a staff member trailing them around the room all evening.

  • Skip straight to the right song. The one her dad asked for, when he asks for it, without anyone having to go and find somebody behind the bar.
  • Cue what is coming next. Line the first dance up while the speeches are still going, so there is no gap and nobody is scrolling a phone in front of a hundred people.
  • Stop the music cleanly. At the end of a song, not halfway through one. The toast lands in a quiet room instead of over an awkward fade.
  • Theirs for the night, not forever. The access is handed over for the evening. The host feels in control, and your system stays yours.

The song controls come with the eJukebox music system - the screen and the music are separate things that work as one. The music side lives at ejukebox.com.au.

AI check

Every photo goes through this before it can reach the wall.

  • AI screens every photo before it can reach the wall. Nudity, offensive gestures, anything tasteless. Held back, not displayed - it never gets as far as the screen for somebody to have to take it down in front of a room.
  • You still have the final say. The manager app approves or hides photos one at a time, or clears a batch in bulk when a table gets carried away.
  • Anything can come down on the spot. A guest who changes their mind tells a staff member and it is off the wall in a tap - one photo, or a whole table's worth in bulk.
  • Push notifications when it needs a person. A glance at the phone, one tap, and it is dealt with from the other side of the room.
  • You can stop the whole show from anywhere. Start it, stop it or change what is on it from a phone, from the car park.
  • The TV itself is locked. Press Escape at the screen and you get a PIN pad - not a desktop, not a file manager, not somebody's browser tabs.
